Modalix SoM · 48-stream detection

Runtime model switching, one shared graph

48 camera streams fan into a small set of detectors held warm on a single MLA. Pointing a camera at a different model doesn't reload anything — it re-wires the graph. Here's the pipeline, and what happens in the ~2 seconds after you click Apply.

What happens when you switch a model

01

Select

Pick one or more cameras in the control panel and choose a model.

host · :8092/control
02

Request

The browser sends the change; the host proxies it straight to the SoM.

POST /api/streams/{id}/models → SoM · :8600
03

Coalesce

The SoM stamps a rebuild request. A worker waits 0.6 s so a burst of clicks becomes a single rebuild.

debounce · one rebuild
04

Re-wire

The fused graph is rebuilt — each camera's decoder is reconnected to its chosen detector. Models are already warm, so nothing loads from disk.

rewire, not reload
05

Resume

The new graph goes live (generation++) and the pump reads its outputs. Cameras blink ~2 s, then run at full rate.

back to 240 fps
⚡

Why it's near-instant. All 2–4 models are compiled and loaded into the MLA once at startup and kept warm. A switch never touches the model — it only re-connects the graph. And there is one shared detector per model, not one per camera, so K models cost K detectors regardless of how many cameras route to them.

Design limits worth knowing

Warm registry

2–4 models loaded at boot. Switching is a graph rewire, so the cost is the rebuild, not a model load.

Route vs. chain

One model per camera (route) holds full 240 fps. Running two models on every camera (chain) doubles MLA work and halves the stream count.

Accurate-model budget

The bf16 model fits ~6 cameras on the single MLA. The panel warns past that; more risks throughput collapse.

The ~2 s blink

Every rebuild re-glitches all cameras briefly. Good for occasional switches; crop toggles are host-side and instant.
